Why do coffee grounds help plants grow?

EcoBean's coffee-ground fertilizer worked in testing, but I wanted to know why. I'm now turning that question into a British Science Association Gold CREST Award project, explaining the result with chemistry at A-level depth.

  1. 2025 · EcoBeanThe first testMixing grounds straight into soil grew mold, so we switched to a coffee-ground "tea" fertilizer. Seedlings fed on day 4 grew far faster by day 5 than a water-only control.
  2. May 2026 · AP Chemistry finalThe chemistryWith my partner Liam, I traced the effect to nutrients, pH, equilibrium, and decomposition kinetics.
  3. Now · Gold CREST AwardThe full projectAround 70 hours of research, testing, and a written report, assessed against the CREST Gold criteria.
See my research log ↗
  • NutrientsUsed grounds carry nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium, plus traces of calcium, magnesium, and iron.
  • Gentle pHBrewing pulls most of the acid into the drink, so used grounds sit near neutral (about 6.5–6.8).
  • Slow releaseSoil microbes break the grounds down and release ammonium; as roots absorb it, equilibrium shifts to release more.
  • Don't overdo itCaffeine at high concentrations can stop seeds germinating, so the dose matters.
